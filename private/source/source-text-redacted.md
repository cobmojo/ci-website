[8] For the first 35 years of my life I was brought up exclusively in churches with the Eternal Conscious Torment (ECT) view of Hell (that is: the wicked/unrighteous who go to Hell will suffer there consciously and actively forever).  I had “come to terms” with it - I knew all the religious philosophy that said why “ongoing, infinite punishment” was suitable, etc., and was not seeking an alternative based on emotional desires.
[9] 
[10]{LINKS=https://youtu.be/nemKBJoCoKI} In November 2022 I was introduced to the Annihilation view of hell via Preston Sprinkle (https://youtu.be/nemKBJoCoKI co-author of Erasing Hell with Francis Chan).  For clarity, Annihilationism has been the name for varying views on the destiny of the wicked, so the view I was introduced to is more precisely called ‘Conditional Immortality’ (CI).  Basically, it boils down to everything I always believed about Eternal Conscious Torment except that the active punishing doesn’t go on for eternity: the wicked are completely wiped out as the culmination of their punishment (which is neither painless nor instantaneous).  I was initially highly skeptical, but after a deep dive into the whole counsel of scripture, I found that CI seems strongly supported.
[11] To be clear: either I was wrong for 30+ years, or I am wrong now (or both), so I appreciate comments or inputs or rebuttals that will help bring me closer to the truth, whatever it is.
[12] 
[13] At the outset of this discussion I must highlight that CI is not an emotional attempt to lessen the punishment to which the wicked are subjected.  Nor does CI imply uniform judgment/punishment any more than ECT implies uniform judgment/punishment  We must firmly agree, whether we end up with the ECT view or the CI view, that God will perfectly judge and punish the wicked - not too little, and not too much (2 Corinthians 5:10 “For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, so that each one may receive what is due for what he has done in the body, whether good or evil.”) - so that the thing in question here is the duration under which He does it.  Is God really incapable of inflicting the appropriate punishment in a finite time? (If you answer “yes” to that, and believe that the punishment for sin must be infinite, take some time reading Roadblock 3).

The Conditional Immortality view is formed by careful reading of scripture, not by philosophical argument. [25] Preface: Why does this matter?
[26]{list=44/0} CI vs ECT speaks to our understanding of the character of God and the fate of the unrighteous. 
[27]{list=44/0} Many people find it hard to reconcile a loving God and ECT, which may hinder their belief.
[28]{list=44/0} Pushback against ECT can spring up false doctrines, e.g. Universalism, post-mortem conversion, as well as unnecessary resistance to discussions like Calvinism/predestination.
[29]{list=44/0} While one might think an intensified view of hell would drive people to Jesus, ECT hinders evangelism because an exaggerated view of hell makes people think they are more likely to go to heaven by default (since hell is so-so-so-so-SO bad that surely only the very worst of humans go there). 
[30] Roadblocks
[31] RB1: “ECT has always been the traditional view, so isn’t CI just some new thing?”
[32]{list=44/0} The writings of most prominent early church fathers show they believed CI.  This includes Clement of Rome (35-99 AD), Ignatius of Antioch (~35-107 AD), and Irenaeus (120-202 AD).  
[33]{list=44/0} CI was apparently the majority view until at least 300 AD.
[34] RB2: Doesn’t being made in the image of God mean we’re eternal beings?
[35]{list=44/0} We aren’t omniscient, omni-present, omnipotent, etc., so why would we be eternal by default?
[36]{list=44/0} The immortality of the soul is from Plato (428-347 B.C.), not the Bible!
[37]{list=44/0} Immortality is specifically listed as something that only God has and is given to us by Christ.
[38] RB3: Doesn’t sin against an infinite God warrant infinite punishment?
[39]{list=19/0} No: MANY verses show that the condition/understanding of the sinner is more important in determining punishment than the one sinned against. 
[40]{list=19/0} Losing out on eternal life could be viewed (from God’s perspective) as an infinite punishment.
[41]{list=19/0} ECT has problems because its required ‘infinite punishment’ is actually never reached, thus God has never administered the proper judgment for sin.
[42] ECT’s “Go-to” passages
[43]{list=36/0} Mark 9:47-48 - Undying worms and unquenchable fire + Daniel 12:2
[44]{list=36/1}  Mark 9:48 is a direct quote from Isaiah 66:24 which refers to the destruction of dead bodies and that neither the worms nor the fire will fail to complete their task of complete destruction of the dead bodies.  There is no indication that these bodies will remain in this same state forever or are actively experiencing suffering, in fact the opposite is implied.
[45]{list=36/1} Daniel 12:2 ‘shame and everlasting contempt’ is probably better translated ‘reproach and everlasting contempt’, both of which are emotions felt towards something, not by something.  So the unrighteous need not be conscious or still present for those emotions to be everlasting.
[46]{list=36/0} Revelation 14:9-11 The torment of the worshipers of the beast
[47]{list=36/1} The smoke of their torment is what rises forever and ever, not the torment itself (multiple other scriptures talk about ‘smoke rising forever and ever’ after complete destruction, not because of ongoing burning - see Isaiah 34:9-10 about Edom and Revelation 19:3 about Babylon).
[48]{list=36/1} The torment can’t go on for eternity, otherwise the angels, who are listed as present for the tormenting, would not get to be in Heaven since they can’t be in two places at once.
[49]{list=36/1} (Besides, this only applies to the worshipers of the beast, therefore it is not the fate of all unrighteous anyways.)
[50]{list=36/0} Lake of Fire & Revelation 20:10-15 “tormented day and night forever and ever”
[51]{list=36/1} The Lake of Fire has a special design for Satan and his angels since it was prepared specifically for them, not for the unrighteous.  
[52]{list=36/1} “Tormented day and night forever and ever” is strategically placed in the text to specifically apply only to Satan, the Beast, and False Prophet, NOT to those thrown in afterwards. 
[53]{list=36/1} Death is destroyed in the lake of fire, so it makes sense the unrighteous are too.
[54]{list=36/0} “Eternal Punishment” / “Eternal Destruction”
[55]{list=36/1} ‘Eternal’ would be better understood as ‘permanent’, not ‘actively ongoing’ (think of Eternal Salvation, Eternal Redemption, Eternal Inheritance, Eternal Judgment, etc.).  
[56]{list=36/1} ‘Punishment’ and ‘Destruction’ are nouns and not verbs (so it doesn’t say ‘Eternal Punishing’).  
[57]{list=36/1} The punishment is destruction.
[58]{list=36/0} The Rich Man and Lazarus 
[59]{list=36/1} The Rich Man’s brothers are still alive therefore he must be suffering in the intermediate state (Hades).  He is not suffering in the lake of fire.
[60]{list=36/0} Weeping and gnashing of teeth and Outer Darkness
[61]{list=36/1} These will occur before and during the Last Judgment but need not continue for eternity.
[62]{list=36/1} ‘Weeping and gnashing of teeth’ is present both in a discussion with a fiery furnace (Matthew 13) and also in discussions without fire (Luke 13, Matthew 22/25).  So the response is not pain from being burned up in a fire and is implied to be occurring at the judgment scene itself.
[63]{list=36/1} ‘Gnashing of teeth’ refers to anger in the Bible, not agony. 
[64]{list=36/1} ‘Weeping’ is crying from sorrow, not due to pain.

[67]{list=36/1} Sin brings death, not torment.

[75]{list=36/1} ‘Perish’, ‘Death’, ‘Pass away’, ‘Does not have life’, ‘Wrath and fury’,  ‘Judgment’, ‘Depart from me’, ‘Destruction’, ‘Eternal Punishment’  ← all of these are consistent with CI, not ECT.
[76]{list=36/0} All the verses that plainly speak of the eternal torment of wicked humans
[77]{list=36/1} If ECT were true it is surprising there isn’t a single verse that plainly says the wicked will consciously be in torment forever and ever (as compared to the vast array of verses talking about the destruction of the wicked).

[92]{list=36/0} Jesus’ death
[93]{list=36/1} Jesus died for us, He didn’t suffer eternal torment for us.  If His suffering paid our debt, then His death would be unnecessary and confusing.

[95]{list=36/1} The day of Christ’s death paints a picture of the fate of the wicked: court, abandoned by the world/friends, conviction, physical suffering, spiritual suffering due to separation from God, death.
[96] The fate of the righteous vs wicked (not opposites!)
[97]{list=36/0} Hell is not the opposite of Heaven!
[98]{list=36/1} The gift of eternal life cannot be compared with the condemnation and death brought by sin.  Eternal life is FAR greater.  It is not the opposite of hell.
[99]{list=36/1; COMMENT_REF=2,3; IN_COMMENT=2,3} Mathematically, the net outcome for humanity is vastly negative if ECT were true and hell is the ongoing, infinite opposite of heaven (since more people are in hell than heaven it would be better for humanity to never have existed).  CI results in a net positive outcome of humanity’s existence since hell is a finite negative experience.
[100]{list=36/0} The unrighteous have no place prepared for them
[101]{list=36/1} Matthew 25 says the righteous will “inherit the kingdom prepared for you” whereas the unrighteous have no place that was prepared for them (good or bad).  They get thrown into the lake of fire which was prepared specifically for the devil and his angels.

[106]{list=36/1; COMMENT_REF=4,5; IN_COMMENT=4,5} God alone is immortal (1 Timothy 6:15-16) but the Holy Spirit makes us spiritually immortal upon conversion (1 Peter 1:23) and Jesus will grant physical immortality to only His followers at the Resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:42-43).
[107]{list=36/1} The unrighteous will not be granted immortality.

[146]{style=Title} Preface: Why does this matter?
[147] In talking with people about this topic, many seem to show signs of disinterest at the outset because they aren’t sure why the debate over Eternal Conscious Torment (ECT) vs Conditional Immortality (CI) matters.  If we are going to spend eternity in Heaven with God, then is this really an important issue for us to get right?  While nothing in this preface is actually evidence for CI being true, here are some reasons why I feel this topic is worth spending time coming to the best understanding that we can:
[148]{list=11/0} Truth matters. We don’t want to say something is true if it is not.
[149]{list=11/0} If God has revealed something to us, then knowing it to the extent He has made it knowable has value.
[150]{list=11/0} The nature of the punishment of the unrighteous reveals to us some aspects of the character of God and our sin.
[151]{list=11/0} For many, ECT does not harmonize with their view of a loving God, which doesn’t inherently make it wrong, but if it is biblically inaccurate it makes the punishment of God out to be something that it is not (specifically something more intense than reality).
[152]{list=11/1} False anticipation of loved ones experiencing ECT. The majority of people ever created will be experiencing the wrath of God (“For the gate is narrow and the way is hard that leads to life, and those who find it are few.” Matthew 7:14).  Yes, we will all be perfectly in agreement with God and His judgments when we get to Heaven, but a false expectation of ECT for people would be quite damaging to our present joy and cause undue distress.
[153]{list=11/1} How can a loving God send people to (ECT) hell? In evangelistic discussions with unbelievers the problem of (ECT) hell and God being good often comes up.  Insisting ECT is true (whether directly or by omitting correction of a false presupposition) can create a cognitive dissonance in the mind of the unbeliever who will find it more difficult to believe and trust the goodness of God.
[154]{list=11/1} Resultant bad doctrine: The idea of ECT in hell seemingly results in the following emotionally driven doctrines (note that many people won’t verbalize these sentiments, but they are likely the driven force behind why they try to make scripture say things it doesn’t):
[155]{list=11/2} Universalism: “I can’t believe ECT in hell for certain/any people, therefore ALL must be saved, despite what scripture may say otherwise”
[156]{list=11/2} Post-mortem conversion: “I can’t believe ECT in hell for people who didn’t have a clear presentation of Jesus & the Gospel, so people must be allowed to have a chance to repent after they die”
[157]{list=11/2; IN_COMMENT=13} A strong resistance to Calvinism/predestination that impedes the ability to weigh the balance of scripture on the topic: “I can’t believe that God would create certain people who are predestined to ECT in hell, therefore everyone must choose to accept or reject God of their own free will.”  Obviously, the Calvinist/Arminianist debate is complicated and involves much of scripture that we won’t dive into here; just pointing out that the ‘barrier to entry’ of ‘people being predestined to hell’ is not what the ECT view would suggest.
[158]{COMMENT_REF=13; IN_COMMENT=13} If CI is true, and people get exactly what (and only what) they deserve for their earthly actions, these visceral reactions to ECT are non-issues.

[160]{list=11/2} Avoiding hell can become the focus of some believers

[166]{list=39/2} The average person (with a very poor understanding of it) isn’t all that excited about ‘forever’ in heaven.  Many people are neutral or only mildly favorable toward heaven vs ceasing to exist.

[191]{style=Heading2} RB1: “ECT has always been the traditional view, so isn’t CI just some new thing?”
[192]{list=9/0} No.  While ECT has been the majority view for the past 1600 years, CI appears to have been the majority view until at least 300 AD. The writings of important early church fathers such as Clement of Rome (35-99 AD), Ignatius of Antioch (~35-107 AD), and Irenaeus (120-202 AD) show they held the Conditional Immortality view (and the way they discussed it indicates that there really wasn’t much debate about it at the time).

He said that in his day “very many” people denied eternal torment. Augustine of Hippo, “Enchiridion,” Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, First Series, Vol. 3, ed. Phillip Schaff (Christian Literature Publishing Co., 1887), pg 273 - top of Chap 112. https://archive.org/details/aselectlibrary03unknuoft
[194]{list=9/1; LINKS=https://www.truthaccordingtoscripture.com/documents/death/immortality-early-church/apostolic-fathers.php} https://www.truthaccordingtoscripture.com/documents/death/immortality-early-church/apostolic-fathers.php has a very detailed walk-through of writings by early church fathers that clearly show support for CI.
[195]{list=9/1; LINKS=https://rethinkinghell.com/2017/09/27/introduction-to-evangelical-conditionalism-the-doctrine-of-eternal-torment-was-not-universal-in-the-early-church/} https://rethinkinghell.com/2017/09/27/introduction-to-evangelical-conditionalism-the-doctrine-of-eternal-torment-was-not-universal-in-the-early-church/ another good resource for evaluating the views of the early church fathers
[196]{list=9/1; LINKS=https://youtu.be/je3AW6QeXzk} Here is a video with a 13 minute overview: https://youtu.be/je3AW6QeXzk [‘Church Fathers who were Conditionalists’].

[304]{style=Heading2} RB3: Doesn’t sin against an infinite God warrant infinite punishment?
[305]{LINKS=https://www.newadvent.org/summa/2087.htm} If you have spent any time in a gospel-preaching church in America, you probably have heard some form of explanation as to why our sins deserve infinite punishment.  At its core, this is a necessary product of the ECT view rather than evidence for it. (Note that ‘infinite punishment’, which implies ‘forever ongoing active punishing’, is very different from ‘eternal punishment’ which is very biblical and discussed in Section 4).  Excluding the inferences made from the ECT interpretation of the primary verses in question, it is difficult to make a biblical case for sin requiring infinite punishment.  You’ll notice that the usual arguments for infinite punishment often lack many scriptural references and are theological/philosophical arguments based on the nature of God.  For example, this quote from Thomas Aquinas is often used (1225-1274 - Summa Theologica https://www.newadvent.org/summa/2087.htm Question 87 Article 4 Objection 2 - the following quote is a variation thereof): 
[306]{COMMENT_REF=16,17; IN_COMMENT=16,17} “The magnitude of the punishment [of sin] matches the magnitude of the sin. Now a sin that is against God is infinite; the higher the person against whom it is committed, the graver the sin—it is more criminal to strike a head of state than a private citizen—and God is of infinite greatness. Therefore an infinite punishment is deserved for a sin committed against Him.” ← What is truly ironic about this quote is that many people use it in a way that Aquinas was not intending.  While Aquinas was a proponent of ECT, if you review the section linked you will see that Aquinas writes this as a possible objection and then himself responds to it. 
[307] Or this similar quote from Jonathan Edwards (1703-1758):
[308] 	“Our obligation to love, honor and obey any being is in proportion to his loveliness, honor and authority. Therefore, sin against God, being a violation of infinite obligations, must be a crime infinitely heinous and so deserving infinite punishment. If there is any evil in sin against God it is infinite evil.”
[309]{LINKS=https://www.gty.org/library/sermons-library/80-376/the-truth-about-hell} Or John MacArthur (2011 - https://www.gty.org/library/sermons-library/80-376/the-truth-about-hell):
[310] “Crimes against the infinitely holy and exalted God are infinitely wicked, and the punishment fits the crime. Infinite crimes against an infinite God deserve an infinite punishment.”
[311] Notice in all cases the lack of scriptural references as well as the immediate jump from ‘God is infinite’ to ‘sin against God requires infinite punishment’.

[375] “And if your eye causes you to stumble, pluck it out. It is better for you to enter the kingdom of God with one eye than to have two eyes and be thrown into hell, where ‘the worms that eat them do not die, and the fire is not quenched.’”
[376]{list=38/1} This directly quotes Isaiah 66:24, which foreshadowed God taking vengeance on His enemies: “And they will go out and look on the dead bodies of those who rebelled against me; the worms that eat them will not die, the fire that burns them will not be quenched, and they will be loathsome to all mankind.” 
[377]{list=38/2} Jesus is quoting this to bring clarity as to what ‘hell’ is.  He is pulling in the context of dead bodies, utter destruction, and scornful contempt to define hell.
[378]{list=38/2} Any Jew hearing Jesus would therefore key in and understand that the eating worms and burning fire are not active elements of inflicting misery on living beings, but rather monuments of complete and utter destruction that are upon DEAD bodies. Thus the implication is that being 'thrown into hell' means that you will become like the bodies in Isaiah 66: DEAD (as in no longer alive) and that the righteous will look on them with scorn (you don’t have to be alive/conscious to be looked upon with scorn - e.g. Hitler). 
[379]{list=38/2} If eternal conscious torment was what was meant by the worms and fire, Jesus would have had to make some big clarifications to the Isaiah 66 passage when pulling it in, but He doesn’t at all.
[380]{list=38/2} Because the righteous look upon them at one point in time (being described here), doesn’t mean that this moment is frozen in time forever (i.e. that we ALWAYS have to be looking at them or that they will ALWAYS be there in that exact state to continue looking upon). Notice Isaiah 66:23 “all flesh shall come to worship before me, declares the Lord.” which is followed by “And they shall go out and look on…”.  So if we are ‘looking on the dead bodies’, then we aren’t worshiping before the Lord (since those are two different places).  So this is probably something we do once.
[381]{list=38/1} Both the worms and the fire have the job of consuming/destroying the bodies 
[382]{list=38/2} The worm feeds on dead flesh, consuming it.
[383]{list=38/2} The word ‘burns’ here implies the act of burning something up, not an experience of pain - see Section 13.  
[384] The language implies that neither will fail to complete its job.  If the worms died, it would mean that the body was not fully eaten.  If the fire was quenched it would mean that the body was not fully turned to ashes.
[385]{list=38/1} ‘Unquenchable’ does not mean ‘will never go out’
[386]{list=38/2} Many fires go out that are not quenched - once they finish burning up their fuel.  Unquenchable just means that it can’t be put out.  A house fire that was unquenchable means the fire-fighters couldn’t put it out before it destroyed the entire house, not that it never stopped burning.
[387]{list=38/2} Ezekiel 20:47-48  “Behold, I am about to kindle a fire in you, and it will consume every green tree in you, as well as every dry tree; the blazing flame will not be quenched and the whole surface from south to north will be burned by it. All flesh will see that I, the Lord, have kindled it; it shall not be quenched.” (see also Jeremiah 17:27 and Amos 5:6) ← Examples of unquenchable fire that is talking about actually destroying things completely.
[388]{list=38/2} Isaiah 1:31 “And the strong shall become tinder, and his work a spark, and both of them shall burn together, with none to quench them.”
[389]{list=38/3} This makes it clear that the ‘strong’ will be burned up, not tortured.
[390]{list=38/2} Jeremiah 4:4 “Circumcise yourselves to the LORD; remove the foreskin of your hearts, O men of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem; lest my wrath go forth like fire, and burn with none to quench it, because of the evil of your deeds.”
[391]{list=38/2} Note that the NIV says in Mark 9:43b ‘the fire that never goes out’ instead of the literal translation from Greek ‘the unquenchable fire’, which as shown above, does not have the same meaning.  The translators clearly presumed ECT when trying to clarify/simplify the language of the text, so one must be careful of using that phrasing as support for ECT!
[392]{list=38/1} Even if the worms and fire last forever (which isn’t probable based on the text and context - see discussion in Section 3.a.iii. about ‘eternal fire’ not lasting forever), ‘undying worms’ would not imply that the person must still be alive. [452]{list=32/2} ‘Eternal destroying’ wouldn’t even make sense!  If the object in question is continuing to exist for eternity, even somewhere in the process of being destroyed, then it clearly isn’t ever truly destroyed (since once something is destroyed it is no longer the original thing in question).  So we would then be required to say that God doesn’t really destroy the wicked (despite the numerous verses that say He does - see Section 8).

[610]{style=Heading2; COMMENT_REF=29; IN_COMMENT=29} 10. Jesus’s words about the fate of the soul and body in hell

[614]{list=25/1} Jesus uses a different word for what will happen in hell (‘destroy’) than what happens on earth (‘kill’).  
[615]{list=25/2} Is Jesus suggesting the outcome in hell is less severe?  Seems unlikely since kill is the strongest word He could pick for what can happen to your body on earth

[846]{style=Heading2} 25. Universalist phrasing kind of makes sense once the wicked are eliminated
[847]{list=22/1} The Universal Reconciliation view of hell suggests that “all” will eventually repent post-final judgment and be saved.  The view appears to ignore many verses about the fate of the unrighteous and hangs its hat on a handful of verses that talk about “all”.  While I believe Universalism to be wrong for a number of reasons, these verses do make more sense in a view where the only humans left in existence are the righteous:
[848]{list=22/2} 1 Corinthians 15:28 “When he has done this, then the Son himself will be made subject to him who put everything under him, so that God may be all in all.”
[849]{list=22/3} If the wicked were still around after final judgment, wouldn’t it make more sense for it to say “God may be all in some”?  Is God really “all” in the unbeliever who has completely rejected Him?
[850]{list=22/2} Ephesians 1:10 “to be put into effect when the times reach their fulfillment—to bring unity to all things in heaven and on earth under Christ.”
[851]{list=22/3} If unbelievers are supposedly in a permanent conscious state of agony, ruin, sinfulness, rejection of and separation from God, is there really “unity” between ALL things?  That would be the ultimate example of disunity!

[895]{style=Heading2} 30. “Is the CI view even punishment if the wicked just cease to exist?”
[896]{list=18/1} The wicked do not just cease to exist upon dying or upon the reading of their wicked deeds.  Conditional Immortality strongly affirms the full and proper punishment of the wicked on the day of judgment. 
[897] 1) Finite experiences matter:
[898]{list=34/0} Based on how we naturally treat animals - which few would argue have an immortal soul - everyone clearly believes that the pain experienced by a finite/mortal creature matters even if that creature may subsequently cease to exist.
[899]{list=34/0} Imagine 2 prisoners on death row.  For his last 24 hours, Prisoner A is treated lavishly and given every creature comfort imaginable.  On the other hand, Prisoner B is tortured horrifically for his last 24 hours.  No one would consider their treatment before death irrelevant.
[900]{list=34/0} All of the situations we and our loved ones presently find ourselves in are finite/limited duration experiences, and yet we do not minimize or write-off the pain that is presently experienced.  Yes, we are able to put it into perspective because of the hope to come, but it doesn’t make it not matter.
[901]{list=34/0} The Bible makes it clear that there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth at the judgment scene (covered in Section #6).  The last judgment is consciously experienced, severe and to be avoided at all costs (Zephaniah 1:15-18, for example).
[902] 2) Exclusion from eternal life with the Lord is the ultimate punishment!
[903]{list=34/0} Would anyone argue that sentencing a criminal to death by administering anesthesia and then a lethal injection would not be punishment since the person experienced no pain?  If pain is the only criteria, then a series of paper cuts would be more painful than death by lethal injection and yet is a much less severe punishment!
[904]{list=34/1} The reason the death penalty is seen as the ‘ultimate’ punishment is not because of the pain inflicted, but because it is the complete and irreversible taking away of what life/goodness would have been left to experience.  In the same way, if the wicked are missing out on joy-filled eternity with their creator, annihilation would clearly fit the bill of ‘eternal punishment’.
