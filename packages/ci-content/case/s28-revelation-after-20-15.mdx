## In brief

Revelation 21:8 names the cowardly, the faithless and the rest, and says their
portion is in the lake that burns with fire and sulfur. Revelation 22:15 says
that dogs and sorcerers and the sexually immoral and murderers and idolaters
are outside. Both sentences fall after the New Jerusalem has come down. If they
are a running report of who is where in the new creation, then the lost are
still in existence long after the lake of fire of Revelation 20, and conditional
immortality (CI) has a serious problem.

This page argues that both passages read most naturally as recapitulation: a
summary of who inherits what, addressed to present readers about present
conduct. It also argues that even a strictly sequential reading does not leave
those outside alive indefinitely, because the thing they are shut out from is
the tree of life.

## Why this is read as continued existence

The objection is a good one, and it deserves to be stated at full strength
before anything is said in reply.

By Revelation 21:8 the vision has already announced a new heaven and a new
earth and the passing of the former things. Revelation 21:8 is not a flashback
tucked into the middle of that announcement; it comes as part of the same
speech from the throne. Revelation 22:15 is stronger still. It does not say
that certain people were destroyed. It says they are outside, and outside is a
location. A location suggests occupants. The list that follows is a list of
people, not of abstractions or of vices in the abstract.

Put those together with Revelation 20:10, where the lake of fire is described
as a place of torment day and night forever and ever, and the traditional
reading has a coherent shape: the lake of fire is a permanent condition rather
than an event that ends, the second death names that condition, and Revelation
22:15 shows us the excluded still there once the city has descended. On this
reading the burden falls on anyone who wants to say that the plainly sequential
book is not sequential at exactly this point.

That is a real argument. No one should wave it away.

## The recapitulation reading

### The tell is in the verse immediately before

Revelation 22:14 pronounces a blessing on those who wash their robes. Washing
robes is something a reader does now. It is not an act performed after the day
of judgment, when the verdict has already been given. So verse 14 is addressing
living people about their present conduct and telling them what that conduct
leads to.

Verse 15 is the other half of the same sentence pattern. If verse 14 describes
present people and their prospect, the most natural reading is that verse 15
describes present people and their prospect too. The passage is sorting the
audience, not surveying the population of the new creation.

### Revelation 21:8 answers Revelation 21:7

The same pattern governs the earlier passage. Revelation 21:7 promises an
inheritance to the one who conquers. Revelation 21:8 sets out the contrasting
portion for those characterised by the opposite. It is a statement about two
classes of present hearers and what each will receive. It is a recap of what
people now described as cowardly and faithless are heading for, which the
vision has already shown in Revelation 20: the second death in the lake of
fire.

## Three further considerations

### The new creation is where righteousness dwells

<Scripture reference="2 Peter 3:13" />

The promise is not that righteousness will occupy one district of the new
creation while the unrighteous occupy the surrounding country. The description
is of the whole thing.

### Even read literally, exclusion from the tree of life is fatal

Suppose someone insists that Revelation 22:15 describes a literal moment at the
opening of the coming kingdom. It still would not stay that way. The emphasis
of the verse falls on the word outside, and what those people are outside of is
a city with the tree of life in it.

<Scripture reference="Revelation 22:1-5" />

<Scripture reference="Revelation 2:7" />

Access to that tree is what the whole of Scripture treats as the condition of
continuing life, from Genesis 3:22-24 onward. Anyone permanently cut off from
it does not go on living permanently. On a literal reading of the geography,
the literal consequence of standing outside is death. That argument is
developed at length in the section on the tree of life.

### A question about where everyone is standing

There is also a picture problem worth naming lightly. If the unrighteous are
simultaneously outside the city gates and in the lake of fire, we are being
asked to imagine the heavenly city descending onto the new earth and coming to
rest on lake-front property. The point is not that the imagery must be tidy.
The point is that a strictly literal and strictly sequential reading of these
verses is harder to hold together than it first appears.

## Those who do not inherit the kingdom

Paul says the same thing in plainer language in three places.

<Scripture reference="1 Corinthians 6:9-10" />

<Scripture reference="Galatians 5:19-21" />

<Scripture reference="Ephesians 5:5" />

Each is a list of the kind of conduct that marks a person now, and each says
what such people will not receive. That is the same move Revelation makes. It
also raises a question worth asking of the traditional reading: is God's future
kingdom bounded by the city walls, or does it cover the whole earth? If it
covers the whole earth, there is no outside left to stand in.

## What this argument establishes

- The verses immediately surrounding both lists address present readers about
  present conduct, which is what a recapitulation looks like.
- Scripture describes the new creation as the place where righteousness dwells,
  without carving out a region for the unrighteous.
- Even on a literal, sequential reading, those outside have no access to the
  tree of life, so the reading does not secure their indefinite survival.

### What it does not establish by itself

Recapitulation is not the only possible reading of these verses, and this page
does not claim that it is. Revelation is a difficult book, and careful
interpreters disagree about how much of it runs in sequence. What the argument
does is remove Revelation 21:8 and 22:15 from the list of passages that settle
the question against conditional immortality, and it leaves the weight of the
case where the rest of this site puts it.
