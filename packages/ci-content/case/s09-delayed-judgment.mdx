## In brief

If the wage of sin is death, an obvious question follows: why is anyone still here?
Adam and Eve were warned that they would die on the day they ate, and they went on to
have children. Unrighteous people live full lives. Scripture’s answer is not that they
cannot be ended, but that God is holding the sentence back while there is still a plan
of redemption running.

That answer cuts both ways. It explains why the unrighteous continue, and it limits how
long they continue: until the day of judgment.

## What Scripture says

Peter states the delay as a deliberate divine act, and gives it a terminus.

<Scripture reference="2 Peter 2:9" />

He repeats it of the created order, using the same phrase about being kept until a named
day.

<Scripture reference="2 Peter 3:7-13" />

Hebrews supplies the other end of the sequence.

<Scripture reference="Hebrews 9:27" />

Genesis 3:22-24 has the same shape at the beginning of the story: the man and woman are
not destroyed on the spot but put outside the reach of the tree of life, treated in
[The Tree of Life and Conditional Life](/case/final-destiny/tree-of-life/).

## The point about continued existence

Human beings do continue to exist between death and judgment. Conditionalism does not
deny this; the passage about the rich man in Luke 16 describes it. What conditionalism
denies is that continuing is evidence of an inherent, unloseable immortality. Peter does
not say the unrighteous cannot be ended. He says they are being kept, and he names the
point at which the keeping stops.

Being kept is the language of something granted and maintained, not of something
possessed in one’s own right. A prisoner held until a trial date is not thereby shown to
be indestructible. On this reading, continued existence before judgment is evidence of
God sustaining, and the reason he sustains is that redemption is still possible. After
the day when all sin is judged, that reason no longer applies.

Defenders of eternal conscious torment (ECT) explain the same facts differently, and it
should be stated plainly: on the traditional view the human soul is immortal by the way
God made it, so no special act of sustaining is needed to explain why anyone continues.
The delay is then about God’s patience toward sinners rather than about the maintenance
of their existence. Nothing here disproves that account. The point is narrower: these
texts describe the unrighteous as being kept, which is language about something given
rather than something owned.
[The Biblical Case for Conditional Immortality](/case/final-destiny/conditional-immortality/)
develops it.

<Callout tone="caution" title="A related point in Irenaeus" as="h3">
Irenaeus makes a closely related argument in the same chapter of Against Heresies where
he discusses continuance as something granted rather than possessed: the creature continues
because God wills it, not because continuing belongs to it by nature. His concern is not
the timing of judgment, so this is a neighbouring argument rather than the same one.
<Cite id="irenaeus-against-heresies" locator="Book II, chapter 34" />
</Callout>

## What this argument establishes

- Scripture describes the unrighteous as kept under punishment until a named day, not
  as continuing indefinitely of their own accord.
- Survival in the intermediate state is attributed in these texts to God keeping them,
  which is a reason for their continuance and also a limit on it.
- The delay has a stated purpose, and the purpose expires at the judgment.

### What it does not establish by itself

- The nature of conscious experience in the intermediate state. These texts do not
  describe it, and this page does not speculate about it.
- That the intermediate state is uniform for everyone who is in it.
- That the soul is not naturally immortal. The positive case is made elsewhere.
