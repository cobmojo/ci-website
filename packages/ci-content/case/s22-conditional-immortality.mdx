## In brief

This is where the threads of the case are drawn together. Conditional immortality (CI) is
the view that unending life is a gift God gives in Christ, and that those who do not
receive it do not have it. Eternal conscious torment (ECT) is the view that the finally
unrighteous exist for ever in conscious suffering.

Four claims carry the argument. Human beings are not immortal by nature. Survival between
death and judgment is God sustaining people, not people sustaining themselves. Those
raised in Christ exchange a perishable body for an imperishable one. And the unrighteous
are never said to receive immortality at any point.

None of the four is established on this page alone. Each is argued elsewhere on the site;
this page shows how they fit together and what follows if they hold.

## Human beings are not immortal by nature

<Scripture reference="1 Timothy 6:15-16" />

Paul says of God that he alone has immortality. Scripture never predicates immortality of
human beings as a standing possession, and treats human life as fragile, derived and
dependent throughout. The full argument is set out in
[RB2, on the image of God](/case/roadblocks/image-of-god/).

The New Testament then locates immortality where the argument would expect it: brought to
light through the gospel in 2 Timothy 1:10, sought and given rather than possessed in
Romans 2:7, and attached to an imperishable inheritance in 1 Peter 1:4 and an imperishable
seed in 1 Peter 1:23.

## Surviving until judgment is God sustaining

The obvious objection is that souls evidently continue after death. The rich man and
Lazarus describes conscious existence after death, and Scripture speaks of the unrighteous
being kept until the day of judgment.

Conditionalism does not deny this. It denies the inference drawn from it. That a person
continues after bodily death shows that God is sustaining that person until the day of
judgment; it does not show that the person is inherently indestructible. The distinction
matters because it is exactly the distinction Scripture itself makes when it says
immortality belongs to God alone. The argument is set out in
[S09, on why final judgment is delayed](/case/biblical-language/delayed-judgment/), and
the passage about the rich man is treated in
[S05](/case/key-texts/rich-man-and-lazarus/).

## Believers exchange the perishable for the imperishable

<Scripture reference="1 Corinthians 15:42-43" />

The verbs are passive throughout. The body is sown perishable and raised imperishable, and
the change happens to the person rather than being drawn out of resources the person
already has. That is the shape of a gift.

## Walking through 1 Corinthians 15

The source document works through the chapter in order, and it is worth following that
order because the argument accumulates.

### Verses 20 to 23

<Scripture reference="1 Corinthians 15:20-28" />

Christ is raised as the firstfruits, and those who belong to him are raised at his coming.
The resurrection in view is defined by belonging to Christ.

### Verses 24 to 28

Then comes the end, when every rule and authority and power is destroyed and all enemies
are put under his feet. The source document presses that image. Being put under someone's
feet is not a picture of living subjection under a new administration. In the prophets it
is a picture of defeat already carried out.

<Scripture reference="Malachi 4:1-3" />

Malachi supplies the image directly: the righteous tread down the wicked, who are ashes
under the soles of their feet. Reading the feet imagery in 1 Corinthians 15 alongside
Malachi 4:3 gives a picture of enemies destroyed rather than enemies alive and subdued.

This is an argument from the way an image is used elsewhere, and images can be used in
more than one way. Defenders of the traditional view read the same phrase as the total
subjugation of every hostile power, with no implication about whether the defeated
continue to exist. That reading is available. The point here is that the Malachi image is
also available, and it is the closer verbal parallel.

### Verses 29 to 36

An extended encouragement to hold fast to the hope of the resurrection: if the dead are
not raised, Paul argues, the Christian life makes no sense.

### Verses 37 to 49

The heavenly body is described as greater than the earthly one: raised in glory, raised in
power, bearing the image of the man of heaven. Verse 43 makes clear that the imperishable
body of verse 42 is the body of those raised in Christ. The whole description is of what
happens to his people.

### Verse 50

<Scripture reference="1 Corinthians 15:50" />

The verse denies that flesh and blood can inherit the kingdom of God, or that the
perishable inherits the imperishable. Inheritance language runs through the chapter, and
it is consistently applied to those in Christ.

### Verse 53

In 1 Corinthians 15:53 the perishable must put on the imperishable and the mortal must put
on immortality. It is stated as something that must happen to those who will be with
Christ. It is never stated of anyone else.

## The chain from body to soul

The source document then draws a short chain. It is set out here in full because it is the
step where the argument turns from what Scripture says to what follows from it.

1. Unbelievers are not described as receiving an imperishable body.
2. Therefore their bodies remain perishable, and perishable bodies die.
3. In Gehenna, body and soul share the same fate, because Jesus says God is able to
   destroy both there.
4. Therefore the soul does not survive what the body undergoes.

<Scripture reference="Matthew 10:28" />

Step three is doing most of the work, and it is not established here. It depends on
reading Matthew 10:28 as tying the fate of body and soul together, and on the sense of the
verb Jesus uses. That is argued at length in
[S10, on whether God can destroy both body and soul](/case/biblical-language/body-and-soul/),
where the traditional reading of the verse as ruin rather than cessation is also stated
and answered. This page assumes the conclusion of S10; it does not assert it
independently.

One consequence is easily misread, so it is worth stating carefully. Conditionalism does
not teach that judgment is painless or instantaneous. Scripture describes darkness,
weeping and gnashing of teeth, and a servant cut in pieces. The claim here is that the
unrighteous do not continue in that condition without end, because they do not continue
without end.

## How ECT interpreters read all this

The traditional response to this synthesis is substantial and should be stated properly.

On immortality, 1 Timothy 6:16 says God alone has immortality in the sense of underived,
essential deathlessness. That is a statement about the kind of immortality God has, not a
denial that God can grant endless existence to creatures. Everyone in this debate agrees
God grants endless existence to the redeemed, which shows the verse cannot mean what a
flat reading would make it mean.

On 1 Corinthians 15, the chapter is about the resurrection of those in Christ from
beginning to end. Paul is not discussing the resurrection of the unrighteous at all.
Silence about their bodies in a passage that is not about them is not evidence, and
Scripture elsewhere teaches a resurrection of both the righteous and the unrighteous, as
in John 5:28-29. If God raises the unrighteous for judgment, he raises them in whatever
condition his purpose requires.

That is a serious answer, and the conditionalist case does not defeat it in one move. The
reply is that the silence is not random. Across the New Testament, unending life,
imperishability and immortality are consistently attached to Christ and to those in him. A
doctrine that requires the unrighteous to be given endless existence has to supply that
from somewhere, and the texts are not there.

## What this argument establishes

- Immortality is presented in Scripture as God's possession and God's gift rather than a
  human property.
- The imperishable body of 1 Corinthians 15 is described for those raised in Christ.
- The unrighteous are nowhere said to be granted immortality or an imperishable body.

### What it does not establish by itself

- The precise nature of the resurrection body of the unrighteous, which Scripture does not
  describe.
- How long the unrighteous consciously exist after being raised. Nothing here settles a
  duration.
- The conclusion of the body-and-soul chain, which rests on S10.

## The question to ask

The source document closes this section with a reframing that is worth keeping. The usual
evangelistic question is where a person will spend eternity. On this reading the prior
question is whether a person will get eternity at all.
